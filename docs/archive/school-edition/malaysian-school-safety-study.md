# Malaysian School Safety and Operations: Implications for AiWAS

## Assessment

**AiWAS addresses part of the visible school-safety problem, but it does not yet cover the full response, safeguarding or operational picture.** Its strongest direction is a school safety and response platform that combines camera events, staff and pupil reports, duty arrangements, inspections and verified follow-up. Expanding the list of AI detector labels alone would leave important Malaysian school needs unmet.

The highest-priority additions are confidential reporting, restricted safeguarding cases, emergency escalation, hostel supervision, verified pupil handovers, facilities inspections and weather/disaster readiness. Daily operational value should come from reducing duplicated reporting and helping staff complete work, not from increasing surveillance of ordinary pupil behaviour. These priorities are analytical recommendations drawn from the evidence below, rather than claims of validated product effectiveness.

The reference period is **1 January 2022 to 11 September 2026**. National evidence and selected reported incidents cover primary, secondary and boarding schools; preschool transport examples are clearly identified where the same handover risks apply. University incidents are excluded from the school case register. Reporting from Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah and Sarawak is included.

This is a broad evidence review, **not an exhaustive census of every reported school incident**. News visibility is uneven, allegations may remain unresolved, and different administrative systems count different populations. There is no defensible “percentage of all Malaysian safety problems solved” that can be calculated from these sources.

## 1. National signals and their limits

### Recorded bullying

| Year | Primary-school cases | Secondary-school cases | Total recorded cases | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2022 | 819 | 3,064 | 3,883 | KPM-school figures cited by the Children’s Commissioner. |
| 2023 | 1,110 | 5,418 | 6,528 | Same published series. |
| 2024 | 1,992 | 5,689 | 7,681 | Same series; subsequently described by MOE as the highest recorded total. |
| 2025 | 740 | 2,149 | 2,889 | Later MOE parliamentary reply reported in February 2026; comparability should be verified before interpreting the fall. |

The 2022–2024 breakdown comes from a September 2025 report; 2025 comes from a later parliamentary response.[^1][^2] The later response attributed the earlier increase partly to awareness and reporting compliance. Therefore, a fall or rise in recorded cases must not automatically be presented as a corresponding change in underlying harm.

**Product implication:** track report volume alongside acknowledgement time, time to protective action, repeat concerns, unresolved cases and pupil confidence in reporting. Do not reward a school simply for having few complaints. An increase after introducing a trusted reporting channel could be a positive discovery signal.

### Health and wellbeing

The NHMS 2022 Adolescent Health Survey reported 8.6% bullied, 13.1% suicidal ideation and 9.5% suicide attempts; the latter two measures refer to the preceding 12 months. Current e-cigarette/vape use was 14.9%. These are self-reported adolescent survey estimates, not confirmed school incident counts or proof that these experiences occurred on school premises.[^3]

**Product implication:** concerns about emotional distress and vaping require confidential support and human assessment. Video must not be used to diagnose mental health, infer intent or label a child as dangerous.

### Other national and sector signals

JBPM reported **76 fires in school areas by 29 July 2025**, with approximately 60% attributed to electrical factors. This is a partial-year count across school areas, not a count restricted to classrooms.[^10] In August 2026, the government reported approval of **367 dilapidated-school redevelopment projects for 2023–2026**; approved projects are not the same as completed works or a complete inventory of unsafe schools.[^12]

MOE reported **8,076 SPM 2024 candidates absent from all written papers as of 6 February 2025**. That examination measure must not be confused with daily truancy or pupils physically missing from campus.[^25] An Auditor-General report described **337 schools still using 4G or satellite service despite completed PoP infrastructure** in the period reviewed; this is historical procurement/connectivity evidence, not a current bandwidth test of every school.[^23]

**Product implication:** fire readiness, asset defects, attendance reconciliation and connectivity resilience deserve explicit workflows. These problems are not solved by a fighting detector.

## 3. Coverage assessment against AiWAS

The PRD describes an intended detection engine and live deployment, but the audited application remains a **fictional, in-memory UI demonstration**. No category below is credited as a validated live service. “Partial” means a screen or workflow exists; it does not mean the school is operationally protected.

Status key: **Partial** = present in the UI but incomplete operationally. **Planned** = PRD/future category, unvalidated. **Gap** = dedicated workflow is absent. Priority **P0** = necessary for a credible safety pilot; **P1** = next high-value module; **P2** = optional or partner-led expansion. Priority is a product judgment, not a national risk ranking.

This matrix contains **33 need areas**, not 33 independently measured risk rates. Several share the same response engine. Building common case, task, permission and notification infrastructure is more valuable than creating 33 disconnected screens.

## 4. The most valuable additions

### A. Confidential reporting and safeguarding

A pupil, guardian or staff member should be able to report without finding a camera event first. Support simple Bahasa Malaysia and English forms, assisted reporting for younger children, and an anonymous option with an honest explanation of its limitations. Anonymous reports need a secure reference or reply channel if follow-up questions are expected.

Create a restricted safeguarding record separate from the general incident log. Store only what the designated team needs, distinguish allegation from verified finding, restrict exports and record every access. If the allegation concerns the normal case owner or a school leader, the workflow should support an approved independent escalation route. Routine product support staff should not have standing access to child-sensitive records.

KPM’s portal already provides reporting materials and the 2023 bullying guideline.[^28] Sexual misconduct has a separate KPM guideline, including complaint handling, criminal/non-criminal case pathways, intervention and reporting.[^8] These distinctions should inform case templates. Proposed AiWAS routing and deadlines must be approved against the applicable current policy, rather than presented as an automatic legal determination.

The 2022 Disrupting Harm report addresses online child sexual exploitation and abuse; its underlying fieldwork predates publication.[^9] This supports an off-camera reporting and referral channel, not blanket scanning of pupils’ messages. Staff should not be encouraged to circulate or repeatedly upload abusive material involving children; specialist evidence-handling procedures are needed.

### B. Hostel operations

Make a hostel view available only to schools that operate boarding facilities. Its working surface should show who is on duty, the latest roll call, pupils with authorised leave, late returns, missed rounds and unresolved welfare checks. Distinguish “not yet checked” from “missing”; the former is an incomplete record, not a confirmed emergency.

A handover should require the outgoing and incoming duty teams to review open high-priority cases. Every welfare event needs discovery time, first response, escalation, clinical handover if applicable and family-contact attempts. A missed round should generate a supervisory task, not automatically imply staff misconduct.

MOE’s 2025 safety programme explicitly included residential institutions and daily hostels, and the later parliamentary response described CCTV and assistant-warden measures.[^36][^2] The product opportunity is to connect human supervision to incident handling. Adding cameras without on-duty ownership leaves a response gap.

### C. Emergency coordination

Separate a **manual emergency activation** from routine evidence review. An authorised person must be able to trigger urgent assistance immediately, even if the AI system is unavailable. Show the location, reported threat, current action owner, acknowledgement and contact status.

Do not require a ten-second analysis cycle before staff can act. Use distinct states: event received, analysis pending, unverified concern, staff acknowledged, response initiated and evidence reviewed. A suspected emergency can receive a protective response before the allegation is finally validated. Conversely, a high model score is not a finding of guilt.

Add evacuation, shelter and reunification workflows only with school-approved procedures. Record headcounts and unresolved people explicitly. A camera-based occupancy estimate can help locate a discrepancy, but it cannot certify that every named child is safe. Medical response instructions and staff training remain outside the competence of a video model.

### D. Facilities and prevention

Expand camera maintenance into a general school asset and hazard register: electrical boards, fire equipment, gates, drains, stairs, railings, sports equipment, toilets and lighting. Allow staff to submit a defect with a location, description and optional non-sensitive photo. Urgent defects need a temporary restriction and an owner; completion needs evidence and appropriate verification.

This is a strong daily-value module because many reported hazards require inspection and repair, not algorithmic classification. Track repeated defects and reopening, not only ticket closure. A contractor marking a repair “done” should not automatically clear a restricted area.

Use planned inspections and event-triggered checks after floods or severe weather. Records should distinguish an observation by a teacher from a competent-person safety clearance. The goalpost case and redevelopment programme show why asset condition belongs in the safety platform, but they do not prove that automated visual inspection can certify buildings.[^11][^12]

### E. Attendance, gates and transport

Model attendance, physical presence and authorised departure as separate concepts. A student can be absent from a class while legitimately receiving treatment, present at school after classes end, or on an approved trip. Keep observation time, source and unresolved discrepancies visible.

Start with teacher-confirmed registers and approved imports, then support gate/transport scans where justified. KPM reporting already describes APDM/eKehadiran and SiPKPM interventions.[^25][^26] No public integration entitlement or supported API was established here; agree an authorised export/import or integration arrangement before promising automatic synchronisation.

For collection, record an authorised collector and an actual handover, not merely a person walking past a camera. For transport, distinguish bus boarding, arrival, alighting and handover; require the operator’s own safety procedures and checks. Avoid promising that school cameras cover incidents along the whole route.

### F. Health, weather and continuity

Create a simple operational calendar that can flag outdoor activities for review when an official weather warning is active. Show when the weather information was last refreshed and whether a human approved, modified or postponed the activity. The March 2026 MOE direction provides a concrete starting reference, but rules should be versioned and locally reviewed.[^15]

Food safety should begin with checklists, supplier/meal records and rapid clustering of staff-reported symptoms. Disease reporting should use minimum necessary health data with restricted access. MOH’s late-2025 bulletin separately reports HFMD outbreaks in schools, preschools and other settings, demonstrating why age group and setting must remain distinct.[^17]

Flood readiness needs alternative teaching/operating arrangements, contact lists, damaged assets and reopening authorisation. The 2026 Paitan communications disruption also matters to a centrally streamed CCTV architecture.[^24] Test how the system behaves when the internet and electricity fail together. A blank dashboard must never silently imply that no incident occurred.

## 5. Operational fit for different Malaysian schools

| School context | Prioritise | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Urban day primary school | Collection authority, gate duty, younger-child reporting, medical assistance and safe play equipment | Treating all pupils as independent phone users |
| Urban secondary school | Confidential reporting, crowd/traffic hotspots, case follow-up and class/presence reconciliation | Equating group gathering with bullying |
| Boarding school | Night duty, welfare rounds, roll call, leave-return and shift handover | Cameras in private sleeping/changing/bathing spaces |
| Rural Sabah/Sarawak school | Connectivity qualification, water/facilities, flood continuity and low-bandwidth workflows | Assuming a published coverage percentage guarantees usable upload bandwidth |
| Private, religious, MARA or other governance context | Confirm institutional authority, policy owner and applicable workflow before onboarding | Assuming one KPM configuration automatically applies to every institution |
| PPKI / pupils needing additional support | Accessible communication, personalised assistance and inclusive emergency planning | Visual inference of disability or automatic punitive flags for atypical movement |

These are deployment recommendations, not claims that any school type has a uniform risk profile. Local walkthroughs and interviews should determine the actual priorities. The PPKI water-activity incident supports careful attention to off-site support planning, not generalisations about disabled pupils.[^21]

## 6. Malaysian policy and ecosystem implications

The current official Anti-Bullying Tribunal portal describes complaint assessment and possible referral to school handling or a tribunal hearing. Its explanation of bullying includes physical, psychological and social harm, with repeated conduct or a single serious incident.[^29][^30] This reinforces why a product definition limited to repeated visible fighting would be too narrow.

BERNAMA reported in July 2026 that KPM was updating guidance in line with the Anti-Bullying Act 2026.[^31] Therefore, use configurable, versioned workflows and retain the policy version used for each case. Before live rollout, obtain confirmation of current duties, reporting deadlines, forms and institutional scope from the responsible authority; this report is not a legal compliance certification.

The government was reported to be **examining** school-CCTV integration with PDRM in October 2025.[^32] That is not evidence that a public API, nationwide operational integration or permission to stream AiWAS footage to police exists. Begin with controlled, authorised evidence export and record the receiving agency and reference number.

For a future government view, default to approved aggregates rather than identifiable pupil histories or unrestricted footage. Show denominator, period, data completeness and reporting-method changes. Do not publish school rankings based simply on low complaint counts. Reporting transparency was explicitly emphasised by the education minister in September 2025.[^33]

## 7. What should change in the PRD

The supplied PRD already recognises that detection confidence is unverified, centralised streaming depends on school connectivity, and facial recognition is excluded. Those are product requirements stated in the document, not independently verified descriptions of Malaysian law. The current UI adds useful reporting and task demonstrations but has not delivered the intended live engine.[^37][^38]

Recommended revisions:

1. **Make response infrastructure a Phase 1 deliverable.** Require authentication, school isolation, durable cases, live notification status, an on-duty owner and evidence access controls before describing the system as operational.
2. **Move camera freshness/tamper monitoring earlier.** A system cannot credibly report safety coverage if it cannot distinguish healthy, stale, obstructed and disconnected feeds.
3. **Separate AI alerts from safeguarding cases.** A case may begin with a child’s report, a maintenance concern or a staff emergency, without a video clip.
4. **Prioritise fire readiness and facilities workflows over low-value visual classifications.** Integration with suitable alarms and human inspections can precede any visual smoke detector.
5. **Treat bullying and weapon detection as evaluated assistance.** Require category-specific false-positive/false-negative evidence under local conditions; a displayed confidence score is not accuracy.
6. **Keep facial recognition out of the first pilot.** It is not needed for the recommended attendance and handover journeys, and it would introduce a distinct biometric data and governance project.
7. **Reconsider appearance-based person-type tags and uniform checks.** They have limited demonstrated value for the safety gaps identified here. Teacher authorisation should come from accounts, badges or visitor records, not appearance.
8. **Reconcile central-only storage with outage requirements.** The PRD prefers office-server processing/storage. Any local buffering or edge processing would be a proposed architecture change needing explicit agreement, not an assumed existing feature.
9. **Define the ten-second target precisely.** Specify whether it is capture-to-analysis or capture-to-delivery; measure median and tail latency, failures and load. Manual emergencies must bypass the analysis wait.
10. **Add burden and quality measures.** Report staff minutes per case, duplicate entry avoided, unacknowledged alerts, reopened cases and unresolved protective actions alongside model metrics.

## 8. Recommended delivery order

### Before a live safety pilot

Implement authenticated staff identities, server-side permissions, durable records and a tested notification pathway. Add manual emergency activation, confidential case access, roster-based ownership and camera health. Provide explicit offline/failure states. A static interface with successful UI tests is not enough.

Run a school-specific survey of camera fields of view, privacy boundaries, lighting, blind spots, power and upstream bandwidth. Agree which scenes can be monitored and which are deliberately excluded. Obtain the school’s actual emergency, safeguarding and reporting procedures and have responsible staff validate the workflow.

### First operational pilot

Use a small set of measurable camera events plus manual reporting, response tracking, facilities defects and a limited set of school-approved checklists. For a boarding-school pilot, add duty/roll-call/handover immediately. For a day-primary pilot, prioritise collection and gate operations instead.

Start in observation/shadow mode for model outputs. Compare alerts against human-reviewed sampled events and documented missed events; do not claim an overall accuracy percentage from confirmed alerts alone. Tune by camera and conditions, while preserving urgent manual access.

### Next modules

Add approved attendance reconciliation, visitor/contractor management, food/cleaning checks, weather decisions and transport records according to each school’s needs. These can share common task, audit and permission services. Avoid expanding to payroll, examinations, fee management or a full school ERP unless a separate business case emerges.

## 9. Pilot acceptance questions and success measures

| Area | Acceptance evidence to request |
|---|---|
| Emergency response | Demonstrate activation without AI, receipt by the on-duty person, escalation when unacknowledged and a complete response timeline. |
| Safeguarding | Demonstrate restricted visibility, an independent escalation route and no sensitive case appearing in a general export. |
| Hostel/day-school operations | Reconcile a missing check, legitimate leave, late return or collection dispute without converting uncertainty into a false fact. |
| Detection | Measure performance per category/camera/day-night condition; include missed events and non-events, not just successful demos. |
| Facilities | Show a defect progressing through restriction, owner action, verification and reopening. |
| Resilience | Exercise lost internet, stale frames, power failure and delayed notification; confirm staff understand reduced coverage. |
| Workload | Compare time and duplicate fields against the existing process; staff should not need to enter the same incident twice. |
| Governance | Identify data owner, access policy, retention, correction, deletion, export authority and incident-response responsibilities. |

Recommended operational measures are acknowledgement time, time to first protective action, percentage of cases with an owner, overdue welfare checks, repeat unresolved defects, confirmed handovers, camera frame freshness and staff time saved. Set targets with the school after baseline measurement. Avoid a target of “zero reports” or a claim that cameras prevented a specific crime without evidence.

## 10. Conclusion

AiWAS is aligned with visible violence detection and incident tracking, but the Malaysian evidence supports a broader and more practical product: **trusted reporting, accountable response and daily school safety operations, supported by carefully evaluated cameras**. The most material missing capabilities are human workflows, not additional visual labels.

The first investment should make the existing platform reliable and usable by the people responsible for safety. Confidential safeguarding, hostel/day-school response, asset inspections and authorised handovers offer a stronger connection to the reported issues than facial recognition or uniform surveillance. A complete live rollout still requires school-specific validation, current-policy confirmation and real service integration.

## Evidence notes

National administrative records, surveys and individual incidents are not interchangeable. Most case-level evidence here is official-agency reporting carried by BERNAMA, supplemented by primary ministry publications and research. Where only a historical report was reviewed, its statement is preserved as historical rather than represented as the latest investigation or court finding.

There is no national exhaustive incident denominator in this study. It cannot establish causal effectiveness of CCTV, demand at a particular price, or the current infrastructure condition of a named prospective customer. Discovery interviews should include school leaders, HEM/safeguarding staff, wardens, counsellors, guards, facilities staff, pupils/guardians, PPD/JPN and relevant technical providers.
